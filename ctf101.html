CTF 101

A Capture-The-Flag (CTF) is a puzzle where the "prize" for solving something is a specific piece of text called a flag, usually formatted like flag{some_text_here}. Your job is to find it, however the challenge requires — reading page source, decoding a cipher, digging through a file, or piecing together clues.

There's no single "right" background for this. Some challenges reward curiosity more than expertise: reading carefully, trying the obvious thing first, and not being afraid to poke at something until it breaks.

How this site works

  • Challenges are grouped into three tracks: Recruit (beginner), Field Agent (intermediate), and Classified (advanced) — pick any track from the home page.
  • Each challenge has a prompt, sometimes an embedded page or downloadable file, and a box at the bottom where you submit the flag.
  • Hints are staged: the next hint only appears after you've opened the one before it. In the Recruit track, hints are always free. In later tracks, revealing a hint costs points.
  • Your progress and score are saved in this browser only (no account needed) — check the Dashboard any time.

Tools you'll want

Most challenges are solvable with your browser alone:

Browser DevTools

Right-click any page → "Inspect" or "View Page Source", or press F12. This is how you read a page's actual HTML/JS/CSS — not just what's rendered on screen.

Built-in decoders

Several challenges include an in-page tool for Base64, hex, XOR, cipher-shifting, and more — no extension installs required.

A notepad

Jot down partial decodes, coordinates, or usernames as you go — multi-step challenges often need you to combine several small findings.

A few tips

  • Read the whole prompt before touching any tool — it usually tells you exactly what to look for.
  • "Hidden" doesn't always mean encrypted — sometimes it just means not visible on screen (an HTML comment, an invisible element, text after a file's official end).
  • Stuck for a while? Open the next hint — in the Recruit track it costs nothing.
  • Flags always look like flag{...} — if your decoded text doesn't look like that yet, you're probably one step away, not on the wrong track.
